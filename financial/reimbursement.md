# Libtayo® (Cemiplimab) Reimbursement and HTA Decisions

## 1. Background
Reimbursement approval from Health Technology Assessment (HTA) bodies is a prerequisite for successful commercialization in countries with single-payer or highly regulated healthcare systems. Because immunotherapies are high-cost medications, agencies like NICE (UK), CADTH (Canada), and PBAC (Australia) perform rigorous comparative clinical and cost-effectiveness analyses. This document reviews reimbursement decisions and clinical criteria for cemiplimab in advanced cSCC.

---

## 2. Key HTA Decisions and Criteria

### 2.1 United Kingdom: NICE
*   **Decision (TA578 - 2019):** The National Institute for Health and Care Excellence (NICE) originally recommended cemiplimab for use within the **Cancer Drugs Fund (CDF)** as an option for treating metastatic or locally advanced cSCC in adults, only if they are not candidates for surgery or radiotherapy.
*   **Transition (2022):** NICE transitioned cemiplimab to **routine NHS commissioning** after clinical trial updates confirmed the long-term durability of overall survival (OS) and complete responses, resolving uncertainty in the initial economic models.
*   **Criteria:** Patients must have advanced disease, not be candidates for local curative modalities, and the company must provide cemiplimab at the confidential discounted price agreed under the Patient Access Scheme (PAS).

### 2.2 Australia: PBAC / PBS
*   **Decision:** The Pharmaceutical Benefits Advisory Committee (PBAC) recommended cemiplimab for listing on the **Pharmaceutical Benefits Scheme (PBS)** under Section 100 arrangements (Efficient Funding of Chemotherapy).
*   **reimbursement Framework:** Listed under **Authority Required** criteria. The PBAC recommended the listing based on a cost-minimisation comparison against existing PD-1 inhibitors (specifically pembrolizumab) already on the PBS schedule.
*   **Criteria:** Eligible patients must have locally advanced or metastatic cSCC and be deemed ineligible for curative surgical resection or radiotherapy by a multidisciplinary team.

### 2.3 Canada: CADTH / pCODR
*   **Decision:** The CADTH Pan-Canadian Oncology Drug Review (pCODR) recommended reimbursement of cemiplimab for advanced cSCC.
*   **Criteria:** Funding is restricted to patients with metastatic or locally advanced cSCC who are not candidates for surgery or radiotherapy, have good performance status (ECOG PS 0–1), and have not progressed on prior immunotherapy.

---

## 3. Payer Challenges and Considerations
High-cost oncology drugs face several reimbursement barriers:
*   **Multidisciplinary Team (MDT) Verification:** Payers often mandate MDT approval to ensure that local modalities (like Mohs surgery or stereotactic body radiotherapy) have been thoroughly evaluated and deemed non-curative before approving systemic cemiplimab.
*   **Budget Impact Thresholds:** HTA bodies evaluate the total impact on the national healthcare budget. The transition of cemiplimab to the adjuvant setting (which involves treating a larger patient pool for a fixed 1-year duration) has triggered new HTA reviews to assess the budget impact.
