# Libtayo® (Cemiplimab) Health Economics and Cost-Effectiveness

## 1. Background
In healthcare resource allocation, demonstrating a drug's clinical efficacy is no longer sufficient; it must also prove cost-effectiveness. Health economic evaluations assess whether the health benefits of a new treatment (measured in life-years or Quality-Adjusted Life Years [QALYs]) justify its additional costs relative to existing therapies. This document reviews published cost-effectiveness and health economic analyses for cemiplimab in advanced cSCC.

---

## 2. Methodology of Economic Evaluations

### 2.1 Model Structure
Published evaluations of cemiplimab typically utilize a **partitioned survival model** comprising three mutually exclusive health states:
1.  **Progression-Free Survival (PFS):** Patients are alive and their disease is stable or responding.
2.  **Progressive Disease (PD):** Patients have experienced disease progression and are receiving subsequent therapies or palliative care.
3.  **Death:** Absorbing state representing overall mortality.

The model typically evaluates costs and QALYs over a **30-year lifetime horizon** to capture long-term survival benefits, with costs and health outcomes discounted at standard rates (e.g., 3.0% in the US, 3.5% in the UK).

### 2.2 Health State Utility Inputs
Health state utilities represent the quality of life associated with a specific disease state, valued from 0 (death) to 1 (perfect health). Trial-based utility data from the EMPOWER-CSCC-1 study (collected via the EORTC QLQ-C30 and mapped to EQ-5D index values) typically yield the following inputs:
*   **PFS State Utility:** **0.805** (reflecting high baseline quality of life maintained during treatment).
*   **Progressive Disease (PD) Utility:** **0.749** (reflecting the decline in quality of life associated with disease progression).
*   **Adverse Event Disutility:** Adjustments are applied to account for the transient reduction in utility during Grade 3–4 adverse events (e.g., pneumonitis, severe colitis).

## 3. Cost-Effectiveness Outcomes and ICERs

### 3.1 United States Perspective
From the US healthcare payer perspective, cemiplimab has been evaluated against chemotherapy and pembrolizumab:
*   **Cemiplimab vs. Chemotherapy:**
    *   **Incremental Cost:** Approximately $150,000–$180,000.
    *   **Incremental Effectiveness:** 1.5–1.8 QALYs gained.
    *   **Incremental Cost-Effectiveness Ratio (ICER):** Estimated at **$99,024 per QALY gained**.
    *   **Conclusion:** Cost-effective at a standard willingness-to-pay (WTP) threshold of $150,000 per QALY.
*   **Cemiplimab vs. Pembrolizumab:**
    *   **ICER:** Estimated at **$130,329 per QALY gained**, reflecting the comparable efficacy but different dosing and list pricing of the two antibodies.

### 3.2 European Perspective
Evaluations from European single-payer perspectives (e.g., Italy, UK) also support cost-effectiveness:
*   **Italy (SSN Perspective):** An analysis comparing cemiplimab to chemotherapy yielded an incremental cost-utility ratio (ICUR) of **€34,110 per QALY gained**, which is well below the national Italian WTP threshold (typically €40,000–€50,000).

---

## 4. Sensitivity and Uncertainty Analyses
*   **Extrapolation of Survival:** The primary driver of uncertainty in cemiplimab economic models is the extrapolation of the tail of the overall survival (OS) curve. Because a subset of advanced cSCC patients achieves long-term complete responses (CR), the assumption regarding whether these patients are cured or continue to face an excess risk of death significantly impacts the ICER.
*   **Drug Price:** Economic models are highly sensitive to the net price of cemiplimab. In countries where confidential discounts are negotiated (PAS in the UK, rebates in the US), the actual cost-effectiveness is superior to evaluations utilizing public list prices.
