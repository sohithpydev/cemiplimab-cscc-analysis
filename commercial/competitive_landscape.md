# Competitive Landscape in Advanced Cutaneous Squamous Cell Carcinoma (cSCC)

## 1. Background
For decades, the treatment of cutaneous squamous cell carcinoma (cSCC) was dominated by local curative modalities. However, for patients who progress to advanced stages (metastatic or locally advanced disease where surgical resection or definitive radiation is no longer curative or feasible), systemic therapies are required. This document reviews the competitive landscape, comparing cemiplimab with historical standards of care and its primary immunotherapy competitor.

---

## 2. Head-to-Head Comparison of Systemic Modalities

### 2.1 Cemiplimab (Libtayo®) vs. Pembrolizumab (Keytruda®)
Pembrolizumab is the only other PD-1 inhibitor approved by the FDA (approved June 2020) for recurrent or metastatic cSCC.
*   **Clinical Efficacy:**
    *   **Cemiplimab (EMPOWER-CSCC-1):** Pooled ORR of **47.2%**, with a high Complete Response (CR) rate of **16.1%**. Durable responses are maintained (median PFS of 26.0 months).
    *   **Pembrolizumab (KEYNOTE-629):** ORR of **50.0%** in the locally advanced cohort and **35.2%** in the recurrent/metastatic cohort. The CR rate is lower (around 6%).
*   **Adjuvant Positioning:** Cemiplimab holds a first-mover advantage in the adjuvant high-risk setting (based on the C-POST trial, showing a **68% recurrence risk reduction**).
*   **Dosing:** Cemiplimab uses a standard 350 mg Q3W fixed dose, whereas pembrolizumab uses a 200 mg Q3W or 400 mg Q6W dose.

### 2.2 Cemiplimab vs. Cetuximab (EGFR Inhibitors)
EGFR is frequently overexpressed in cSCC. Cetuximab is used off-label in patients ineligible for immunotherapy.
*   **Clinical Efficacy:** Cetuximab shows an ORR of **28% to 36%**, but responses are transient, with a median Progression-Free Survival (PFS) of only **4.1 months**.
*   **Tolerability:** Cetuximab is associated with a severe, highly visible acneiform rash in >90% of patients, reducing compliance and quality of life.

### 2.3 Cemiplimab vs. Platinum Chemotherapy
Historical standard of care for advanced disease (e.g., cisplatin/carboplatin + 5-FU).
*   **Clinical Efficacy:** ORR ranges from **30% to 50%**, but responses are brief and rarely complete. Myelosuppression and cumulative toxicities limit long-term use.
*   **Safety Profile:** High rate of Grade 3–4 toxicities (>50%), including nephrotoxicity, neurotoxicity, and hematologic suppression, which is poorly tolerated in the typical elderly cSCC patient population.
