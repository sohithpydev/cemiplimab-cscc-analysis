# Cemiplimab Post-Marketing Safety Surveillance and Pharmacovigilance

## 1. Background
While clinical trials provide the initial safety evaluation, they are limited by sample size and follow-up duration. Pharmacovigilance utilizes post-marketing safety surveillance databases, such as the FDA Adverse Event Reporting System (FAERS) and the EMA’s EudraVigilance, to detect rare, late-onset, or newly recognized adverse events (safety signals) in the general population.

---

## 2. FAERS Safety Signals and Disproportionality

Recent disproportionality analyses of post-marketing FAERS reports for cemiplimab have identified several key safety signals:

### 2.1 Immune-Related Myocarditis and Pericarditis
*   **Safety Signal:** Although extremely rare (occurring in <1% of patients in clinical trials), myocarditis and pericarditis exhibit a high Proportional Reporting Ratio (PRR) in FAERS disproportionality analyses.
*   **Clinical Presentation:** ICI-induced myocarditis presents with chest pain, palpitations, dyspnea, and elevated troponin levels. It is characterized by rapid onset (often within the first 2–6 weeks of therapy) and has a high mortality rate (30% to 50%), requiring immediate hospitalization, permanent drug discontinuation, and high-dose methylprednisolone.

### 2.2 Severe Cutaneous Adverse Reactions (SCARs)
*   **Safety Signal:** Rare reports of life-threatening skin toxicities have been logged:
    *   **Stevens-Johnson Syndrome (SJS)**
    *   **Toxic Epidermal Necrolysis (TEN)**
    *   **Drug Reaction with Eosinophilia and Systemic Symptoms (DRESS)**
*   **Management:** Requires immediate suspension of cemiplimab and transfer of the patient to a burn unit or specialized dermatology center.

### 2.3 Nephritis and Renal Dysfunction
*   **Safety Signal:** Post-market reports have highlighted immune-mediated nephritis, presenting as an asymptomatic increase in serum creatinine. Routine renal function monitoring is mandatory before each cemiplimab cycle.

---

## 3. FDA and EMA Label Modifications
In response to post-marketing safety databases, regulatory agencies have updated the official cemiplimab prescribing labels:
*   **Myocarditis Warnings:** The warnings and precautions section was updated to include detailed guidance on monitoring and management of immune-mediated myocarditis. The label mandates permanent discontinuation of cemiplimab for Grades 2, 3, or 4 myocarditis.
*   **Toxicity Grading Tables:** Prescribing tables have been adjusted to provide clear thresholds for holding or permanently discontinuing the drug for specific renal, cutaneous, or cardiac events.
