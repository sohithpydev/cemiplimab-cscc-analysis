# Global Adoption and Referral Patterns for Cemiplimab in cSCC

## 1. Background
The commercial success of cemiplimab (Libtayo®) depends not only on clinical data but also on structural healthcare dynamics. Monoclonal antibody immunotherapies require specialized administration (intravenous infusion) and active safety monitoring. Understanding physician referral pathways, geographic utilization differences, and access barriers is key to evaluating the drug’s real-world adoption.

---

## 2. Physician Referral Pathways and Adoption

The treatment of cSCC is highly interdisciplinary, involving dermatologists, Mohs micrographic surgeons, radiation oncologists, and medical oncologists.

### 2.1 The Referral Loop
*   **Dermatologists / Mohs Surgeons:** Typically the first point of contact, managing the surgical resection of primary and recurrent lesions.
*   **The Transition:** When a tumor becomes unresectable, metastatic, or exhibits high-risk features where surgery would cause severe disfigurement, the patient must be referred to a medical oncologist.
*   **Adoption Hurdles:** Historically, dermatologists had limited interactions with medical oncologists regarding non-melanoma skin cancer, as it was considered a localized surgical disease. Regeneron has focused commercial efforts on creating robust interdisciplinary **multidisciplinary tumor boards (MDTs)** to facilitate early referral and avoid delayed initiation of systemic therapy.

### 2.2 Oncologist Adoption
*   **Guideline Integration:** Rapid uptake among medical oncologists was driven by the early inclusion of cemiplimab as a preferred systemic agent (Category 2A preferred) in NCCN and ESMO guidelines.
*   **infusion Infrastructure:** Oncologists are comfortable with anti-PD-1 therapy due to prior experience with pembrolizumab and nivolumab, removing any barriers to prescribing cemiplimab.

---

## 3. Geographic Disparities and Access Barriers

*   **United States:** Libtayo has achieved high penetration (90%+ market share in cSCC), supported by private insurance coverage, Medicare reimbursement, and patient assistance programs (Libtayo Surround®).
*   **Europe:** Uptake varies by country based on national HTA reimbursement negotiations. In the UK, transition from the Cancer Drugs Fund to routine commissioning has stabilized access.
*   **Developing Nations and Access Barriers:** 
    *   **Cost Barrier:** The high cost of cemiplimab (~$11,700/vial in the US, £4,650 in the UK) makes it inaccessible in low-and-middle-income countries (LMICs) without public subsidy.
    *   **Infusion Infrastructure:** PD-1 inhibitors require sterile infusion centers, cold-chain drug storage, and oncologists trained to manage severe immune-related reactions. The lack of this specialized infrastructure in rural or developing regions prevents clinical adoption.

---

## 4. Remaining Unmet Clinical Needs
Despite the success of cemiplimab, several unmet needs persist:
1.  **Immunocompromised Patients:** SOTRs face a high risk of allograft rejection under PD-1 blockade. Safe, graft-sparing systemic immunotherapies represent a major unmet need.
2.  **PD-1 Refractory Disease:** Approximately 50% of advanced cSCC patients exhibit primary resistance to cemiplimab, and some acquire resistance. Currently, there are no approved targeted therapies or next-line systemic options for these patients.
