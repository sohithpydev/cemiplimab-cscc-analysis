# Research Glossary of Terms

This glossary defines key clinical, pharmacological, intellectual property, and financial terms used across this project.

---

## 1. Clinical and Pharmacological Terms
*   **adjuvant Therapy:** Treatment given after the primary therapy (usually surgery) to lower the risk of the cancer returning.
*   **neoadjuvant Therapy:** Treatment given before the primary treatment (usually surgery) to shrink the tumor and make resection easier or less disfiguring.
*   **Objective Response Rate (ORR):** The proportion of patients who achieve a partial response (PR) or complete response (CR) under a therapeutic regimen.
*   **Complete Response (CR):** The complete disappearance of all detectable cancer lesions.
*   **Progression-Free Survival (PFS):** The length of time during and after treatment that a patient lives with the disease but it does not get worse.
*   **Overall Survival (OS):** The length of time from either the date of diagnosis or the start of treatment that patients are still alive.
*   **Disease-Free Survival (DFS):** The length of time after treatment during which no cancer is found.
*   **Kd (Dissociation Constant):** A measure of the affinity of a drug for its target receptor. A lower Kd indicates higher binding affinity.

---

## 2. Biomarkers and Precision Medicine Terms
*   **Tumor Mutational Burden (TMB):** A measurement of the number of somatic mutations present in a tumor's DNA. Usually expressed as mutations per Megabase (mut/Mb).
*   **Signature 7:** A specific pattern of somatic DNA mutations characterized by C>T transitions at dipyrimidine sites, directly caused by exposure to ultraviolet (UV) radiation.
*   **Tumor Immune Microenvironment (TIME):** The cellular and molecular components surrounding a tumor, including immune cells (T-cells, dendritic cells, macrophages) and signaling molecules (IFN-gamma).
*   **ctDNA (Circulating Tumor DNA):** Tumor DNA circulating freely in the patient's bloodstream, used as a liquid biopsy marker.

---

## 3. Intellectual Property and Regulatory Terms
*   **BPCIA (Biologics Price Competition and Innovation Act):** A US federal law that established an abbreviated licensure pathway for biological products shown to be biosimilar to an FDA-licensed reference biological product.
*   **Regulatory Data Exclusivity:** A period during which a competitor cannot rely on the reference drug's safety and efficacy data to license a biosimilar (12 years in the US, 10 years in the EU).
*   **Evergreening:** A commercial strategy where pharmaceutical companies secure additional patents (e.g., formulations, dosing, methods of use) to extend monopoly protection as core patents near expiration.
*   **RTOR (Real-Time Oncology Review):** An FDA program designed to expedite the review of oncology applications by allowing early submission of clinical data.

---

## 4. Financial and Health Economics Terms
*   **Wholesale Acquisition Cost (WAC):** The manufacturer's list price for a drug to wholesalers or direct purchasers, not including discounts or rebates.
*   **HTA (Health Technology Assessment):** A systematic evaluation of properties, effects, and/or impacts of health technology (e.g., NICE, PBAC).
*   **Incremental Cost-Effectiveness Ratio (ICER):** A metric used in cost-effectiveness analysis to summarize the cost utility of a therapeutic intervention compared to an alternative ($/QALY).
*   **QALY (Quality-Adjusted Life Year):** A measure of the state of health of a person or class in which the benefits, in terms of length of life, are adjusted to reflect the quality of life.
