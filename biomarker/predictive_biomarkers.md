# Emerging Biomarkers and Resistance Mechanisms in cSCC Cemiplimab Therapy

## 1. Emerging Biomarkers
Due to the limitations of PD-L1 immunohistochemistry (IHC) and Tumor Mutational Burden (TMB) as standalone predictors, several novel biomarker platforms are undergoing active evaluation in advanced cutaneous squamous cell carcinoma (cSCC).

### 1.1 Circulating Tumor DNA (ctDNA) Dynamics
*   **Concept:** ctDNA refers to fragmented tumor-derived DNA circulating in cell-free plasma. It serves as a real-time, non-invasive liquid biopsy.
*   **Clinical Efficacy:** While baseline ctDNA quantity correlates with tumor burden, the *dynamic change* during treatment is highly predictive. Studies show that patients who achieve **complete ctDNA clearance** by week 6 or 9 of cemiplimab treatment have a significantly higher rate of durable Disease-Free Survival (DFS) compared to those with persistent ctDNA, making it a valuable tool for monitoring response in both advanced and adjuvant settings.

### 1.2 T-Cell Receptor (TCR) Repertoire and Clonality
*   **Concept:** NGS of the TCR $\beta$ chain CDR3 region allows characterization of T-cell diversity and clonal expansion.
*   **Clinical Evidence:** Responders to cemiplimab often exhibit oligoclonal T-cell expansion post-treatment—reflecting the proliferation of specific T-cell clones targeted against tumor neoantigens. In contrast, non-responders show either a lack of T-cell expansion or persistent polyclonal activation without focus.

### 1.3 Transcriptomic Gene Expression Signatures (GEPs)
*   **Concept:** Profiling the expression of specific gene sets using multiplex PCR or RNA-seq.
*   **Signatures:** Signatures capturing T-cell inflamed phenotypes (e.g., chemokine gene subsets *CXCL9*, *CXCL10*; cytolytic markers *GZMA*, *PRF1*; and immune checkpoint molecules *PDCD1*, *CD274*) show stronger correlation with cemiplimab clinical benefit than PD-L1 expression alone.

## 2. Mechanisms of Resistance to Cemiplimab

Despite high initial response rates, approximately 50% of advanced cSCC patients exhibit primary resistance, and a subset of responders eventually develops acquired resistance.

### 2.1 Primary Resistance (De Novo Resistance)
Primary resistance occurs when a tumor fails to respond from the initiation of therapy.
*   **Lack of Immunogenicity:** A subset of tumors, despite UV exposure, may lack immunogenic clonal neoantigens or express them at low levels, preventing T-cell recognition.
*   **Severe Immunosuppression:** Active recruitment of myeloid-derived suppressor cells (MDSCs), M2 macrophages, and high expression of TGF-$\beta$ can physically exclude T-cells from the tumor core or induce T-cell dysfunction that cannot be reversed by PD-1 blockade alone.

### 2.2 Acquired Resistance
Acquired resistance occurs when a patient who initially achieved a clinical response (partial or complete) subsequently experiences disease progression.

#### 2.2.1 Loss of Antigen Presentation
Tumors can escape immune surveillance by disabling the machinery required to present neoantigens to T-cells:
*   **B2M Mutations:** Somatic mutations or loss of heterozygosity in the $\beta_2$-microglobulin (*B2M*) gene prevent assembly and transport of MHC Class I molecules to the cell surface, rendering the tumor cell "invisible" to cytotoxic CD8+ T-cells.
*   **HLA Downregulation:** Epigenetic silencing or genetic deletion of specific HLA alleles (e.g., HLA-A, HLA-B) impairs presentation of immunodominant neoantigens.

#### 2.2.2 Alterations in Interferon-Gamma (IFN-$\gamma$) Signaling
The IFN-$\gamma$ pathway is critical for upregulating MHC expression, recruiting immune cells, and mediating tumor cell apoptosis.
*   **JAK1/JAK2 Mutations:** Loss-of-function mutations in Janus Kinase 1 (*JAK1*) or Janus Kinase 2 (*JAK2*) disrupt the JAK-STAT signaling cascade downstream of the IFN-$\gamma$ receptor. 
*   **Consequences:** Tumor cells with *JAK1/2* mutations become insensitive to the cytolytic effects of IFN-$\gamma$ and fail to upregulate MHC Class I expression in response to immune activation, driving therapy evasion.
